It still paints in the upper deck's stair slot, but its group moves by min(main lift, upper lift) − upper lift, so it rides the lower of the two decks: the foot never leaves the main floor and the landing never rises above the upper floor. Painter's order holds for any downward offset of the stair against the upper deck: below the floor plane the stair can only be covered by the front well piece/near grid (painted after), never by the back piece. Step 3 changes
- Spiral stair (replaces the straight flight; step-2 build kept at work/build-step2.mjs): the standard A380 rear stair. Central column (kit lathe: base flange, a hub collar under each tread and under the landing, cap), 13 wedge treads of 25.7° each (π/7, so exactly 7 treads fill the half behind the column) winding counter-clockwise, 14 risers of 0.196 m (2.75 m total), tread radius 0.82 m, outer helical stringer 0.82–0.85 m (outside diameter 1.7 m), a baluster per tread, a round handrail (kit tube, cut at every tread boundary) 0.9 m above the treads with newels at both ends, nosing strip per tread. Landing: a three-tread-wide sector at the upper floor with a guard along its open radial edge. Round well (0.89 m radius) cut between the floor beams at frames 1 and 4, trimmers at |y| = 0.91 m, a lining drum down to the beam line, guard ring at 0.92 m (toe board, posts one tread apart, top rail) open over the landing. Everything still rides in the upper-deck group, so the stair rises with the upper deck when exploded.
- Painter's order of the stair: items split at the column silhouette (view azimuth ± 90°): back-half treads (2–8) → column → front-half treads (0, 1, 9–12) and the landing. Within each half the order is a topological sort of a pairwise test: overlapping sectors paint lowest first (landing over treads 0–1); disjoint sectors are split by a line through the column and the far side paints first. The well's floor panel is two pieces split along the same silhouette line (back piece before the stair, front after); the seam has no outline.
- Seats restored: main-deck centre row 3 (front) is back; centre rows 1–2 stay out (stair footprint). Upper-deck centre block stays clear round the well (landing area; seats there hid the well). Seat tracks are cut round the stair/guard ring.
- Camera: the drawing is fitted to the assembled section (pad 74); a second fit includes the exploded upper deck (pad 60). The whole drawing sits in one <g data-view> whose affine transform eases between the two fits on a critically damped spring (ω ≈ 7.2/s) that starts with the explode, so it zooms out while the upper deck climbs and back in when it closes. Strokes are non-scaling, so line weights do not change. Rest fills ~64% of the card height, exploded ~67%.
- Hover lift reduced to 0.10 m (upper, main) and 0.08 m (cargo): the floor beams stay alongside their frame fittings, so a picked deck never floats above the stub tops.
- Bins: each far-side bin is carried on square struts from its own floor (feet at the floor edge, top at the bin's underside; four in the legroom between rows plus one at the front end, visible ahead of every seat). Main-deck struts slide inboard with their bin. The main-deck centre bin hangs from the upper floor and now moves with the upper deck (it paints in the main-deck slot, offset by the difference of the two decks' lifts), so its hangers never float.

Stair dimensions: no published numbers. Sources only say the rear stair is spiral and narrow (FlightGlobal 2008; airliners.net quoting an Airbus guide: "if you met someone coming down, you would both have to turn sideways"; Simple Flying 2026: central column supports the steps). Diameter 1.7 m, tread angle and count are schematic, chosen inside common spiral-stair sizes (60–72 in) and to fit between two frames; the rise 2.75 m is the drawn deck spacing (5.30 − 2.55 m).
